Houston, TX

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Houston, advised by Jaspal Subhlok and Thamar Solorio (external). My research spans multimodal large language models and vision-language models, Theory of Mind, embodied reasoning, and AI for education. I am a recipient of the UH Presidential Fellowship and the Cullen Fellowship Travel Grant, and expect to graduate in January 2028.

I build AI systems that ground their answers in verifiable evidence. My first-author paper, Cite or Decline (EMNLP 2026, Industry Track), introduces a course-grounded chatbot for STEM lecture videos with chapter-guided retrieval and timestamped citations. In a semester-long deployment on VideoPoints, we evaluated 833 messages and observed no cross-course retrieval. The system improved correct-lecture retrieval by 6.3 percentage points over a dense-only baseline on EduVidQA.

I also contributed to MoMentS (Findings of EMNLP 2025), a multimodal Theory of Mind benchmark with more than 2,300 questions across seven categories. I oversaw the technical work, built the annotation platform, guided the annotation team at McGill University, and ran model evaluations with vision and audio. My ongoing work explores perception-grounded Theory of Mind for robotic reasoning and multimodal deception detection.

Previously, I was a Senior Engineer in Advanced Research at bKash Limited, a mobile financial service with more than 70 million verified users. I developed LLM chatbots, GraphRAG systems, card OCR, and face anti-spoofing models, with a focus on efficient production deployment.

I am available for Summer 2027 internships and open to relocation. You can download my resume or reach me at sahmed75@uh.edu.
